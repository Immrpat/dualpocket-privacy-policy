DualPocket: NDS Emulator for Android

Privacy Policy

Effective November 13, 2026

This policy applies to DualPocket: NDS Emulator (also labeled NDS Emu in earlier builds), Android package com.namib.ndsemu, developed by Immrpat. The emulator runs offline and does not require an account.

Files and data on your device

  • The app reads the game files you select through Android’s file picker. It can remember the names and Android file references of up to eight recent games and keep read access so you can reopen them.
  • If you choose to import an ARM7 BIOS file, the app stores a copy in its local storage for emulation.
  • The app stores in-game saves, save states, display and control layouts, and emulator preferences on your device. It processes touch, button, and gamepad input locally to run the game.

Collection and sharing

The emulator does not send your files, saves, settings, or gameplay data to the developer or other parties. It has no advertising, analytics, or developer-operated cloud sync.

Google Play handles downloads and any purchase of the app under Google’s Privacy Policy. DualPocket does not receive your payment card details. If you select a file from a cloud storage provider in the Android file picker, that provider handles access under its own policies.

Retention and deletion

The app keeps saves, the imported BIOS, and settings in Android’s app-private storage. Recent game entries are replaced as new ones are added. You can delete app-held data in Android Settings by opening the app’s storage settings and choosing Clear storage, or by uninstalling the app. This removes local saves and settings. Your original files remain in the storage location you selected and can be managed there.

Visiting this page

This page uses no analytics or tracking scripts. GitHub Pages hosts it and may log your IP address when you visit. See GitHub’s privacy statement.

Privacy questions and requests

Contact the developer through the DualPocket privacy policy issue tracker. GitHub issues are public, so please leave out personal information, game files, BIOS files, and save files.
